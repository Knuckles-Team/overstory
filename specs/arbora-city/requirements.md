| `OVERSTORY-CITY-R003` | **Live fleet through the host.** The host route `GET /api/apps/city/fleet` calls the served Arbora operation `fleet.catalog.list` as the bound actor and returns a strict normalized shape: per server — name, served tool count, `packStatus`. The browser never contacts Arbora directly. The host caches the read for 60 s, keyed by the verified principal and tenant — no caller is ever served another caller's view. A cold read is single flight: concurrent cold boots share one in-flight read instead of repeating the fan-out, the read runs as one real asyncio task with the waiters awaiting it through `asyncio.shield` (a cancelled caller never strands the shared read or cancels it for everyone), and the in-flight entry is cleared in `finally` so no state survives a failure. The whole fresh read — catalog listing plus the per-pack tool-name fan-out — is bounded by an overall 30 s deadline; exceeding it answers the unavailable 503 with a stated reason. The catalog read paginates by cursor (`limit: 100`, following `next_cursor`, safety cap ten pages) so a paged catalog is never truncated. The per-pack tool-name fan-out runs concurrently under a concurrency cap (8 in-flight upstream reads) with a per-call timeout (5 s), filtered by server (`servers: [name]`, `kinds: ['tool']`, `limit: 12`) rather than free text; a typed refusal or timeout on one pack degrades that pack's tool list to empty and the rest of the read stands, while an absent operation or unreachable engine fails the whole read with the stated 503 and any other typed refusal passes its code through. Caller chain: `GET /api/apps/city/fleet` → `build_city_router` in `agent/overstory/apps/city/router.py:50` → `CityGateway.fleet()` in `agent/overstory/apps/city/gateway.py:118` → the injected host invoke (`served` operation, wired in `default_apps()` of `agent/overstory/apps/__init__.py`) → the served operation `fleet.catalog.list`. Shape: `agent/overstory/apps/city/router.py` (reuse `host.invoke_operation` through the injected `invoke` callable, the `_ERROR_STATUS` mapping and `AppBackend` registration in `agent/overstory/apps/__init__.py`); the city app is `available` — it degrades to `SIMULATED` (R014) instead of hiding. Depends: none. | `agent/overstory/__tests__/test_city_app.py::test_the_fleet_route_serves_the_whitelisted_catalog` passes (catalog shape over a fake gateway, 503 on unavailable, 60 s principal-scoped cache, cancellation-safe single-flight cold boot, cursor pagination of the catalog read, 30 s overall fresh-read deadline, per-pack typed refusal degrades only that pack, bounded concurrent fan-out with per-call timeout, status route, typed refusal passes through). |

| `OVERSTORY-CITY-R013` | **Pinned asset verification.** Every shipped character asset is pinned in `public/city-assets/assets.lock.json` with the source commit, sha256 and byte count. The gate is honoured at render time as well as at startup: the renderer consumes only verified, pinned bytes, and a refused or hash-mismatched pin renders a labelled placeholder and never falls through to an unverified fetch. The lockfile carries a per-file `license` and a top-level `license_allowlist`; startup verification refuses a pin whose license is outside the allowlist — licensing is a gate, not a note. Every locked asset also passes automated QA against the stated budgets (triangles ≤ 4000, materials ≤ 16, texture size ≤ 2048 px, bounds within the stated range) with a rendered turnaround thumbnail on file for human review; a failing asset never enters the lock. Generated assets (R024) record the generator model's licence terms beside the hosting repository's. Caller chain: startup verification in `src/apps/city/components/CityPage.tsx` → `pinBytes`/`pinnedBytes` in `src/apps/city/core/assets.ts` → placeholder fallback in `src/apps/city/core/render3d.ts`. ## Production-city quality amendment

### `OVERSTORY-CITY-R048` — Procedural regional world, visual quality and performance integrity

| Requirement | Required behavior | Planned evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `OVERSTORY-CITY-R048` | Deliver the procedural regional world. Rollup for R048.1 through R048.9 and the criteria below. | All child acceptance and the combined reference scene pass. |

The default 3D experience SHALL be a deterministic regional-world recipe, not a repeated test grid. One `worldSeed` produces terrain, hydrology, roads, parcels, zoning, vegetation, district massing and material variation. The recipe MUST preserve a flat serviced basin beneath the civic city, then form a regional landscape outside it; a city layout generator MUST reject invalid output before render.

- **Regional composition.** The map represents an expandable 9×9-tile regional world with dense core, mid-density districts, suburbs, industrial/service areas, rural/natural edge, waterfront and river/canal/drainage. Terrain, water and transport corridors constrain district placement.
- **Urban form.** The core includes high-rise/skyscraper massing; suburbs contain low-rise homes, yards and local streets; rural tiles contain terrain, vegetation and sparse structures. Vacant reserved plots use compact state beacons, never plot-sized translucent overlays or repeated decorative squares.
- **Architecture and materials.** Buildings are watertight volumes with connected walls, windows and roofs. At least six façade families vary deterministically by district, archetype and seed; palette, roughness, window rhythm, roof form, setbacks and height vary. A single default white/grey façade or ground field fails the visual gate.
- **Roads and public realm.** Roads use ownership-safe segments and explicit junction assets. Segments end at junction boundaries, junctions own their fill surface, and road surfaces may not be coplanar. Crossings are compact, correctly oriented perpendicular to travel, restricted to appropriate arms, and never overlap. Curbs, sidewalks, street furniture, vegetation and plazas honour parcel and road-clearance rules.
- **Collision and lifecycle validation.** Generation validates parcel overlap, road clearance, terrain slope, water exclusion, junction ownership, façade/roof/wall connection and prop clearance. Invalid recipes are refused with structured diagnostics before render. Regenerated textures and temporary resources are pooled, bounded or disposed on replacement.
- **Scale and agents.** One city metre is authoritative: people render at 1.6–1.9 m, doors/storeys match that scale, and all AI and simulated-human characters retain a visible focus affordance at city zoom without becoming building-sized.
- **Measured performance.** Reference 1× desktop p95 is ≤16.7 ms after 15 minutes of demo activity and planned-capacity onboarding; integrated-profile low tier is ≤33.3 ms. Draw calls, texture bytes and scene-object count may not grow in a 10-minute no-onboarding soak. Distant massing is instanced/LOD’d; high-detail kits are reserved for selected/near districts.
- **Visual evidence.** Approved browser captures cover dense core, suburban street, waterfront/canal and skyline in day/night. Regression rejects bland materials, missing volumes, plot-overlay checkerboards, overlapping roads/crosswalks, carriageway props, wrong actor scale and resource/frame-time growth.
- **Climate and mood.** Arbora's default world reads as vibrant Caribbean/coastal urbanism: clear blue sky, warm direct sun, turquoise shallow water, saturated tropical foliage, bright cloud layers and crisp distance—not polluted grey smog. Fog may establish depth but may not desaturate the civic basin or obscure the skyline.

Depends: R028, R031, R032, R041.1 and R047. Verification: deterministic world recipe tests, geometry/collision audit, long-running soak metrics and approved browser evidence.
